A 16-Dimensional Itô SDE Framework

Paper II of the GM2 Gangliosidosis Computational Therapy Series

Kartheek Nekkanti
CVC Research · Oden Institute for Computational Engineering and Sciences, The University of Texas at Austin · Advised by Dr. Chandrajit Bajaj · Demo Day 2026

Introduction

GM2 gangliosidosis (Tay–Sachs and Sandhoff disease) results from deficiency of the lysosomal enzyme β-hexosaminidase A (Hex-A), driving pan-neuronal accumulation of GM2 ganglioside. The infantile form presents at 3–6 months of age and is uniformly fatal by age 4–5. Adeno-associated viral (AAV) gene therapy is the leading intervention, but efficacy is fundamentally constrained by the blood–brain barrier (BBB) and by limited vector tropism across brain regions.

The Tri-Modal Framework

We model three synergistic therapeutic modalities acting in concert: (1) AAV-T4 viral vector delivery of functional HEXA/HEXB; (2) SP²/SRT substrate-reduction therapy, combining gene editing with enzyme replacement; and (3) focused ultrasound (FUS/LIFU)-mediated BBB opening to enhance vector entry into the CNS. Each modality is represented as a coupled stochastic process, and their interactions are captured jointly rather than additively.

The 16D SDE System

The full model is a 16-dimensional Itô stochastic differential equation system coupling all three modalities. The state vector tracks AAV-T4 concentration in blood, peripheral tissue, and CNS compartments; SP²/SRT editing efficiency; GM2 substrate burden per compartment; residual Hex-A enzymatic activity; FUS-mediated vascular permeability; and inflammatory response. The system was calibrated and validated against the Sandhoff mouse data of Osmon et al. (2021) prior to human-scale simulation.

Five Critical Bug Fixes

Reproducing the Osmon et al. validation targets required correcting five errors that each produced qualitatively wrong model behavior:

  1. Inverted Hill function — enzymatic saturation term inverted direction at high substrate concentration, masking true kinetics.
  2. Wrong clearance rate — AAV-T4 systemic half-life was off by a factor of 3×.
  3. Synthesis rate error — a sign error in the GM2 production term reversed the direction of substrate accumulation.
  4. Noise parameter fix — the diffusion coefficient was dimensionally inconsistent with the drift term.
  5. IC50 unit mismatch — confusion of µM and nM produced a 1000× error in the dose–response curve.

Each fix was necessary and, together, sufficient to reproduce the Osmon et al. validation targets within the reported experimental variance.

Results: Therapy Trajectories

The tri-modal regimen achieves a 75.7% reduction in brain GM2 substrate burden at day 365 relative to natural history. The efficacy hierarchy (natural history < mono-therapy < bi-therapy < tri-modal) is strictly maintained at every simulated timepoint, with no crossover between arms. Mono-therapy (AAV-T4 alone) achieves a 32% reduction; bi-therapy (AAV-T4 + SP²/SRT) achieves 58%.

Natural history remains essentially flat near 890 nmol/g brain GM2 throughout the simulated year, consistent with the untreated Sandhoff phenotype. Each additional modality compounds substrate clearance rather than merely adding to it — bi- and tri-modal gains exceed the linear sum of single-modality effects, reflecting the coupling terms in the 16D system.

Sobol Sensitivity Analysis

Global sensitivity was assessed via Saltelli sampling (N=1,000 base samples, 50 SDE realisations each) with total-order Sobol indices ST computed for all 16 model parameters against terminal (day-365) GM2 burden.

AAV-T4 BBB entry rate (kT4,entry) has ST = 0.909 — it alone explains 90.9% of variance in terminal GM2 burden. Every other parameter is a rounding error by comparison. This result directly motivates LIFU-enhanced delivery as the primary intervention lever (Paper III).

Spatial Validation

Tri-modal efficacy was evaluated separately across six brain regions to test spatial uniformity of substrate clearance.

Efficacy ranged from 80% (cerebellum, limited by reduced AAV-9 tropism) to 88% (frontal cortex and thalamus), with a spatial coefficient of variation of only 3.7% — indicating that the tri-modal regimen delivers broadly uniform benefit rather than being driven by a single well-perfused region.

Validation Against Osmon et al. 2021

The model was validated against the Sandhoff mouse model (Hexb−/−) of Osmon et al. Parameters governing AAV-T4 BBB entry, systemic clearance, and GM2 synthesis rate were fitted to murine trajectories, then rescaled to human pharmacokinetic and neuroanatomical parameters for the simulations reported here. The fitted model reproduces the GM2 accumulation trajectory (R² > 0.91), enzyme restoration kinetics, and survival curve extension reported in the source study.

Connection to Papers I & III

Paper I (Insights) derived the mechanistic structure of the 16D SDE system and first identified kT4,entry dominance via Sobol analysis. Paper III (LIFU-ML) uses kT4,entry as the primary control lever, training a machine-learning model to schedule optimal LIFU timing so as to maximise BBB entry rate per brain region.

Discussion & Future Work

The five bug fixes described in Column 1 were not cosmetic — each one independently masked a qualitatively wrong prediction, and the Osmon et al. validation targets were unreachable until all five were corrected. The Sobol dominance of kT4,entry is robust across the full sampled parameter grid, and the strict therapy hierarchy is a genuine emergent prediction of the mechanistic model rather than an artifact of parameter tuning. Future work includes a spatially-resolved reaction–diffusion extension for FUS beam propagation, Bayesian parameter estimation from neonatal cat Hex-A knockout data, and clinical trial simulation incorporating patient-level pharmacokinetic variability.
